# Monetary execution and control-ledger integration

Status: qualification implementation, not deployed or enabled for live money. Founder-approved policy `orio-final-monetary-2026-11-13-v1` applies. External review remains advisory. This implementation does not change cloud spending/access authorization.

## Executed contracts

`ORIOMonetaryController` deploys its immutable ORIO currency, reserve vault and redemption-lock manager. Only the controller can mint/burn; the token has 18 decimals and no protocol transfer fee. No administrator can set the monetary price or sweep backing. Company withdrawals are limited to separately recognized conversion fees. Transfers cannot spend locked redemption balances.

The controller starts disabled, rejects Base mainnet deployment, and requires the registry to bind its exact controller/vault before governance can enable it. Governance is the configured timelock. It controls qualified valuation signer, settlement operator, migration adapters, configuration revisions, company withdrawals and zero-supply reconciliation. Interface checks are sanity checks, not proof of authentic custody or governance; deployment must pin code, constructor inputs and approved roles.

Valuations use standard EIP-712 with chain/controller identity, asset, USD36 value, registry epoch/revision, controller revision, validity window of at most 300 seconds, issuance permission and evidence digest. Redemption signatures additionally bind the sealed epoch, common opening version/price, immutable request ID, inclusion ordinal, owner, recipient, quantity, minimum payout, nonce and deadline. Local tests compare both hashes with separate standard EIP-712 tooling.

Purchase collection, appreciation, fee segregation, mint and checkpoint commit in one transaction. Redemptions freeze snapshot and membership, require signed token locks, burn only locked authorizations, pay the exact native proceeds and apply the remaining-holder allocation atomically. Any payout failure rolls back burns, consumption, custody and state version. Unconsumed locks can be released by anyone after epoch completion or permissionless expiry; request/nonce replay remains prohibited. A participant who never supplies a valid lock can still cause an epoch to expire: durable admission, lock-before-inclusion workflow, fair platform ordinals and abuse controls remain required.

The vault checks exact incoming/outgoing balance deltas; fee-on-transfer assets are unsupported. Reserve replacement swaps only backing, retains company fees in their original asset, records actual valuation changes, verifies remaining liabilities and completes registry activation in the same controller transaction. A bound registry rejects activation before the vault has replaced its master, and rejects abort after physical replacement. No supply, holder balance, conversion fee or reference-price change occurs during replacement. The trusted swap adapter, price/risk evidence and actual asset behavior still require qualification.

The price is integer USD36 per ORIO. Token liabilities and exact rounding obligations use USD54 internally; master precision is limited to 18 decimals in this contract implementation. Unsafe arithmetic/precision reverts. No residual allocation threshold or appreciation escrow is introduced. Full redemption retains the reference and blocks issuance until governance records zero-supply reconciliation. That checkpoint is accountable governance evidence; the contract cannot independently inspect off-chain legal or accounting obligations.

## Finalized accounting importer

`MonetarySettlement` is a trusted owner-only, read-only-chain importer with no HTTP route, signing or broadcast capability. Migrations 012–015 have no runtime grants and are local only. Initialization supports an original empty qualification deployment, not arbitrary supply import.

Two configured sources must agree on canonical finalized evidence. Each purchase/redemption proof pins controller/currency/vault/master, operation, version, exact checkpoint encoding, allocation events, burns/mints and native payouts/collections. Event matching is bounded by that controller call's start/checkpoint markers; unrelated operations in an EntryPoint bundle cannot supply its funds. Distinct source names alone do not establish operational independence.

The importer replays the separate TypeScript monetary engine and compares every fee/allocation and resulting supply, price, backing, rounding, phase and cycle. Historical replay times are arithmetic-only inputs, not current valuation authorization. It then locks the deployment head and commits shared receipt ownership, asset-balanced journals, immutable execution evidence and new version in one database transaction. Concurrent identical imports return the same journal. Conflicting versions, evidence, missing transitions and already consumed receipts reject without partial journal/claim updates.

Finalized zero-supply reconciliation advances the same version stream using the retained reference. It requires an explicit state-bound reconciliation record, matching reserve, zero pending redemption/company-allocation assertions and the exact digest emitted by governance. Those assertions are a trusted accountable-adapter boundary, not proof inferred from the contract event. No assets move and no artificial zero journal is created; immutable checkpoint evidence and receipt ownership still commit atomically. Tests run full redemption, reconciliation and reopening through the actual contract and database.

Master accounts distinguish reserve cash/backing obligations from company cash/revenue. ORIO accounts distinguish issuance control and each canonical owner's balance. Ordinary finalized non-mint/non-burn transfers move ownership between those accounts without a conversion fee or supply change. Self transfers have immutable evidence without a monetary journal. USD54 rounding entries use the explicitly separate `USD54_MONETARY_MEMO` statistical unit; they are not spendable USD cash and must not be added again to reserve assets in consolidated reporting.

Post-journal checks require exact reserve and company cash agreement with the contract checkpoint. Unobserved company withdrawals therefore stop import rather than silently overstate cash. The company-withdrawal observer requires matching vault-start and controller-completion events, with exactly one matching cash movement inside that call's boundaries. It atomically records company distributions and reduces company cash without touching customer reserve or backing accounts. Receipt replay returns the same journal; a redemption or unrelated bundled payment cannot satisfy the bounded withdrawal proof. Historical-master company withdrawals are accepted only for assets in this deployment's immutable election history. Ledger balances are reconciled observations, not authorization to spend on-chain assets.

## Verification

`npm test` in contracts executes actual signed transactions and synthetic token transfers in the pinned local EVM, including multi-wallet batches, tiny residual supply, reopen, authorization/replay, deposit-only pause, migration loss rejection, transfer locks, expiry release, source divergence and failed payout rollback. Thirty-five chained real transitions compare against the TypeScript engine. Compilation enforces actual runtime/init-code size limits rather than relaxing VM limits.

`npm run test:local` in control-foundation creates a disposable PostgreSQL database and tests the complete import path with real VM event bytes. It covers concurrent retry, deliberately failed journal insertion after receipt acquisition, rollback, duplicate cross-module ownership, transfer then redemption by the recipient, immutable evidence, hash-chain verification, and rejection of unobserved company withdrawals. The EntryPoint integration also executes a real batched approval/purchase through the account/paymaster, imports the actual vault/mint checkpoint and reconciles separately reserved user gas and unused-gas credit. Funding, finality anchors and RPC sources are synthetic fixtures; the offline VM has no Base L1 publication fees.

## Remaining integration and qualification

Production remains disabled. Still required: automatic canonical chain indexing/backfill and transfer completeness; conversion/redemption intent lifecycle adapters for the existing preparation workflows; authoritative zero-obligation audit generation; historical account migration; qualified oracle/risk policies and custody adapters; consumer signing/recovery and gas quotation UX; full Base cost attribution and actual paymaster/bundler/provider qualification; operational reconciliation, restoration and incident rehearsals; target infrastructure and accountable release-specific legal/custody/reserve/security/operations acceptance.

The importer deliberately stops at unhandled controller lifecycle versions and election changes. It cannot import receipts already credited by the older master-entry path: shared receipt ownership prevents duplicate assets. A production dispatcher must select exactly one adapter per economic event and coordinate the existing intent states, rather than run both accounting paths on the same receipt. No local test proves a public deployment, exhaustive economic attack resistance or a completed financial platform.

## Finalized master replacement continuation

The migration observer now binds the actual controller reserve replacement and registry election inside one finalized execution. It checks the previous/new master identities and both retained old-company cash and new backing/company cash, preserves supply/reference/rounding/cycle/phase, rejects unsupported liabilities, and records exact native reserve replacement plus separate USD54_MIGRATION_MEMO loss/gain entries. The signed valuation control in this statistical unit may be negative; no cash account may become negative. Actual ownership conservation is enforced by prohibiting ORIO transfer events inside the known controller migration, not by treating a supply digest as a customer ownership proof.

Receipt claims, journals, monetary version, updated pinned master configuration and immutable election history commit together. A new service instance uses the returned configuration. Identical old-instance migration retries return the existing result. Empty original-genesis replacement is supported before first issuance without inventing a zero journal. Tests exercise gain then covered loss, missing election evidence, omitted old-company withdrawal causing complete rollback until observed, historical fee withdrawal and empty-genesis migration followed by issuance. Continuous canonical indexing and existing intent-lifecycle adapters are still outstanding.
